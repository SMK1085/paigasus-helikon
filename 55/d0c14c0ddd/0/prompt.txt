The invariant that makes it work: after draining a batch, `cursor = slice.next_cursor`, and `read_from` computes `next_cursor = max(cursor, first_seq) + events.len()` = total-appended-count at read time. The next read clamps `effective_cursor = max(cursor, first_seq) ≥ cursor`, so the next batch starts at or after the previous batch's end — **no overlap (no re-emit) and no skip of any still-retained event**. The only skipped seqs are `[cursor, first_seq)`, which are exactly the evicted ones — the documented eviction gap. The old `cursor.max(first_seq) + 1` per-event step produced the same seq progression, just one event at a time.
